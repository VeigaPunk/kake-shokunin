# Review221 — WCAG 2.2.1 Timing Adjustable

**Decision: Disagree — the card is substantially source-grounded, but its evidence instructions leave criteria-specific gaps and one phrase implies exclusivity.** Review was limited to this card and the pinned 2024-12-12 WCAG 2.2 source.

## Findings

### Medium — evidence does not require proof that Turn off or Adjust works, or that Adjust reaches the required range

- **Card field / substring:** `evidenceNeeded`: “showing the turn-off or adjust control is reachable before the limit is encountered”
- **Source anchor:** `#timing-adjustable`, Turn off and Adjust options. Turn off requires the user be allowed to turn off the limit before encountering it; Adjust requires the user be allowed to adjust it before encountering it over a wide range at least ten times the default setting.
- **Reason:** Reachability alone does not show that activating Turn off actually disables the time limit, that Adjust changes the limit, or that the available range reaches ten times the default. The preceding `publisherCheck` names the range, and the `limitation` correctly says a reviewer must establish that controls work, but this evidence instruction does not require recording those outcomes. A reachable but ineffective control or an adjustment with an inadequate range could therefore be recorded as sufficient evidence.
- **Minimal correction:** Require the walkthrough to operate each control and record its effect; for Adjust, record the default and available adjusted duration/range and verify the range is at least ten times the default.

### Medium — exception evidence does not state the linked glossary tests

- **Card field / substring:** `evidenceNeeded`: “real-time event with no possible alternative, essential limit whose extension would invalidate the activity”
- **Source anchors:** `#timing-adjustable` Real-time Exception and Essential Exception; `#dfn-real-time-events`; `#dfn-essential`.
- **Reason:** The source defines a real-time event as an event that occurs at the same time as viewing **and** is not completely generated by the content. “Essential” has its own conjunctive definition: removing it would fundamentally change the content’s information or functionality, **and** that information/functionality cannot be achieved another conforming way. The evidence wording asks for a written justification but does not say to establish these definition conditions; relying on ordinary meanings of “real-time” or “essential” could support an exception the source does not permit.
- **Minimal correction:** For a real-time claim, require evidence for both definition prongs as well as that the limit is required and no alternative is possible. For an essential claim, require evidence for both glossary prongs as well as that extending the limit would invalidate the activity.

### Low — “single option” suggests mutually exclusive outcomes

- **Card field / substring:** `publisherCheck`: “record which single option applies”
- **Source anchor:** `#timing-adjustable`, opening sentence: “at least one of the following is true”. The listed options can overlap (for example, a limit longer than 20 hours may also be adjustable or turn-off-able).
- **Reason:** The criterion requires at least one route, not exactly one applicable route. The card may intend that the reviewer record one sufficient basis, but “which single option applies” can be read as requiring the options to be mutually exclusive or only one to be true.
- **Minimal correction:** Say “record at least one option that applies (more than one may apply).”

## Checks that agree

- The card has exact `id` `2.2.1`, title `Timing Adjustable`, level `A`, and the pinned source URL ending in `#timing-adjustable`.
- `sourceQuote` (“For each time limit that is set by the content, at least one of the following is true”) is a case-sensitive 18-word quote present in this criterion’s `normativeText`.
- All six criterion routes are represented: Turn off, Adjust, Extend, Real-time Exception, Essential Exception, and 20 Hour Exception. Their stated thresholds/conditions and the example’s “longer than 20 hours” wording match the source.
- The 15-minute inactivity timeout is a plausible failure example given the stated absence of all ordinary routes and exceptions. The limitation explicitly says source/automated review cannot establish a working mechanism or exception, and the card makes no website conformance claim. `status` is `not-assessed`.
- The criterion’s note says to consider SC 2.2.1 with SC 3.2.1 regarding changes resulting from user action. The card does not contradict that note; the omission is not necessary to correct the scoped evidence issues above.
