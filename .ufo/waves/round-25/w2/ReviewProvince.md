## Explicit tension, not an inferred execution incident

E2–E4 establish a sound global boundary. E5–E8 nevertheless place imperative execution procedures inside a field called `publisherCheck`; 4.1.3 alone locally states the separate-authorization handoff. Reading cards under the packet workflow reconciles the wording: they are future review specifications, not authority. Reading a detached card can lose that distinction. **[INFERENCE]** This is a portability/ownership ambiguity, not evidence that kake actually drove a browser, accessed secrets, evaluated a property, or violated its round scope.

Do not resolve the ambiguity by deleting authentication, transaction or runtime-dependent criteria: that would damage fixed-population preparation coverage. Conversely, “human reviewer” or “benign test data” cannot substitute for separate authorization. These are distinct constraints, not values to average into a score.

## One proposed move: carry the existing boundary into each card

Generalize the already-used 4.1.3 preparation/request pattern within the existing `publisherCheck` and `limitation` prose, without adding fields or changing the fixed population:

1. Identify what kake prepares from authorized publisher material: component/process inventory, source references, questions and evidence request.
2. Present rendering, keyboard/pointer, device and AT procedures as requirements **for a separately authorized evaluator**, not instructions for this packet to execute. Route browser operation to the browser owner; do not treat that routing as granting authorization.
3. Where accounts/authentication/transactions or stored-data changes are involved, require an operator-approved evaluation scope and credential-custody arrangement before any execution. Keep secrets and private authentication artifacts out of the packet; collect only the necessary non-secret behavior description. If authority is absent, record an unmet evidence need in the separate work item, not a pass or inapplicability result.

This is a proposed consistent card-writing convention, not a new evaluator, runtime, security task, credential workflow, conformance engine or machine authorization gate. Use E4's existing build/date/page/process/method evidence requirements and E8's 4.1.3 wording as the local patterns.
