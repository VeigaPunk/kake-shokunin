## Statements
1. Why the 75th percentile is used — majority of visits.
   (a) Classification uses the 75th percentile of visits so most visits experienced the target
       level; a higher percentile satisfies this better but is more outlier-prone.
   (b) Quote (24 words): "By using the 75th percentile, we know that most visits to the site
       (3 of 4) experienced the target level of performance or better."
   (c) Anchor: "Choice of percentile"
2. Why the 75th percentile is used — outlier resistance.
   (a) The 75th-percentile value is less affected by outliers than a higher percentile: for 100
       visits, 25 anomalous samples are needed to move it (versus 5 at the 95th percentile).
   (b) Quote (12 words): "the 75th percentile value is less likely to be affected by outliers."
   (c) Anchor: "Choice of percentile"
3. What "meets the threshold" means for a page or site.
   (a) A page or site is classified "good" for a metric when at least 75% of its page views meet
       the good threshold; conversely, if at least 25% meet the poor threshold, it is "poor".
   (b) Quote (23 words): "if at least 75 percent of page views to a site meet the \"good\"
       threshold, the site is classified as having \"good\" performance"
   (c) Anchor: "Refresher: Core Web Vitals metrics and thresholds"
4. The classification unit is the page/site aggregate of page views.
   (a) "Meets the threshold" is evaluated on the 75th percentile value of all page views to that
       page or site, not on a single visit or page.
   (b) Quote (25 words): "to classify the overall performance of a page or site, we use the 75th
       percentile value of all page views to that page or site"
   (c) Anchor: "Refresher: Core Web Vitals metrics and thresholds"
5. How mobile and desktop are assessed — same thresholds.
   (a) Mobile and desktop differ in device capabilities and network reliability, but user
       expectations do not depend on device, so the recommended thresholds are shared and a site
       gets one classification for both.
   (b) Quote (19 words): "the Core Web Vitals recommended thresholds are not segregated by device
       and the same threshold is used for both."
   (c) Anchor: "Whether to have the same or different criteria per device"
6. How mobile and desktop are assessed — thresholds are mobile-set.
   (a) Because mobile devices are more constrained, most thresholds are set on mobile
       achievability rather than a true joint threshold; the page deems this acceptable since
       mobile is the majority of traffic for most sites.
   (b) Quote (20 words): "The more constrained nature of mobile devices means that most of the
       thresholds are therefore set based on mobile achievability."
   (c) Anchor: "Whether to have the same or different criteria per device"
7. What "origin" means versus page/site.
   (a) An origin appears only as the unit of the achievability verification: a candidate "good"
       threshold is valid only if at least 10% of CrUX origins meet it.
   (b) Quote (12 words): "we require that at least 10% of origins meet the \"good\" threshold"
   (c) Anchor: "Achievable by existing web content"
