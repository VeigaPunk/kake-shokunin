Attack on the axis naming of C1/C2/C3 (round contract §9: name the axis by the surface the round changes). ## C1 AXIS: calibration (declared) stands, with two measurement defects that must be worded/bounded

Verdict: calibration is the better fit; the operability reading fails the surface test.

Reasons:
1. AXES.md calibration is "Self-assessment quality: gaps named honestly, confidence graded, overreach caught" with signal "SHONEN.md/SCORECARD entries match observed repo state". C1's changed surface is exactly the lane's self-assessment artifacts: the SCORECARD `## Round 57` measured-at statement, the three after-receipts, and the new `receipt-errata.json` disclosure of six historical relabels. The counter (`c57_receipt_pins.py`) compares the SCORECARD section against receipt revision lines and against git history (`undisclosed_relabels`, `phantom_errata`, `scorecard_pin`).
2. The operability signal ("a checklist item can be run and produces the stated outcome") does not fit: C1 verifies no command's printed outcome. The object is a stated pin vs committed history, not a runnable artifact. Round-56 C1 was operability-shaped (documented tally vs selftest print); round-57 C1 is not.
3. Bound that must be in the report: the "observed state" is committed receipt text + git history, not the world. The docstring and contract explicitly do not test "that a counter was actually run at the pinned revision". So the claim is self-report-vs-committed-history, not self-report-vs-reality. Saying "calibration: SCORECARD matches observed state" without this bound overstates.
4. Defect A (scorecard_pin is a substring test): the counter's `scorecard_pin` is `measured_rev[:12] in section` — any occurrence of the pin in the `## Round 57` section passes, not a "measured at" statement. Proven counterexample in a scratch repo (mktemp, `git init`, selftest identity): the SCORECARD section literally said "measured at 462b888e3c91" (wrong revision) while mentioning the true measured revision `f2ac57e2ee17` once in an unrelated sentence; the counter returned `new_receipts=3 scorecard_pin=1 met=True`. The C1 counter therefore certifies a "pin" that is not the section's measurement statement — the calibration surface is not actually measured as stated.
5. Defect B (causal entanglement): C1's predicate requires `new_receipts == 3` — C1's met demands that C2's and C3's after-receipts exist. AXES.md: "Claims must be causally separable — each verifiable without the others having landed." C1 as frozen cannot verify unless C2 and C3 also land; if C2 or C3 fails, C1 fails regardless of its own surface. This entangles three axes in one predicate.
