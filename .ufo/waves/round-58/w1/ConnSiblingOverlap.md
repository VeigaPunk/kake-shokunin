## CONTESTED:

1. **Receipt-identity semantics (C1 vs kumitate)**: kumitate owns the canonical-JCS record-identity definition and nonce-ledger attestation for its own receipts (`kumitate-receipt-attest-v3`). If c58's "receipt rewrite" notion is formalized, "rewritten X" is defined by kumitate's record-vs-byte identity line, not by kake; an uncoordinated definition creates two divergent identity standards. No lane claims the right to audit kake's history on kake's behalf.
2. **Cross-lane registry reads (C2)**: kake's id_resolver reads sibling registries and emits `owners=`/`url-match=` verdicts. The read itself matches established fleet practice (kagi, tabi, kotoba all read sibling/tree registries read-only, pinned). Contested points are format assumptions: the resolver parses `corpus/SOURCES.md` under an exact `## Registry` heading with first-cell `S###` rows (`id_resolver.py:14-18,39-45`), so kama's `registry/*.tsv` and tabi/kagi's row shapes would be invisible → false `unowned`/`dangling` labels about sibling data. Sibling registeries are each owned by their lane; kake must not impose a format or imply ownership.
3. **Crawler-side vs publisher-side (C3 vs tabi)**: declared split, but declared one-sidedly by kake; tabi's client-side half is its own ground and any C3 claim about *crawler behavior* (what a crawler does) borders tabi's client-side discipline — the C3 claims are identification entries (what the crawler is), which kake's `crawler-identity.md` precedent already scopes to publisher-side verification. The reverse/forward-DNS half of crawler identification has no declared owner anywhere.
