RECURRING CLASSES:

- **Carried defects:** settlement/disposition and counter-gate shortcomings persist across adjacent rounds; round 58 explicitly labels the crawler-identity sentence carried since round 51. Some defects become newly framed descendants rather than exact same predicates, so do not treat related wording as proven identical closure state.
- **Receipt rewrites / provenance:** round 52 replayability/fidelity; round 56 relabeled receipts after the run; round 57 repeats the prewritten revision/exit placeholder pattern. The common failure is that committed receipt identity/revision does not faithfully prove the represented execution. No final closure is evidenced by round 58.
- **Stale pins / measurement drift:** round 51 measured commit descriptive rather than hashed; round 52 final receipt header differs from “measured tip”; round 56 revision labels changed without rerunning changed counters; round 57 a pinned gate goes red on later tree and needs historical/revision pinning. A continuous provenance-risk class, but individual root causes differ.
- **Quote fidelity / corpus truth:** round 51 mirror/entry quote conditions; round 52 dangling cross-registry IDs, bad locators and false refusal grounds; round 53 S374 qualifier and CJK length; round 54 unresolved CJK ceiling; rounds 55–56 quote ceiling/rejection-ground scrutiny; round 58 crawler-identity stale assertion. Broadly recurrent grounding/fidelity concerns, not one unchanged defect.
- **Settlement/wave accounting:** missing 53 seat dispositions recurs in 54, and round 55 flags dead/misrouted seats. Wave counts and shape issues (50, 54) are adjacent accounting/conformance problems.
- **Important distinction:** “not routed again” is not proof of closure. The series often marks a defect open, carried, or status-neutral; this table reports only explicit closure cues or later non-reporting.

VERDICT: The strongest recurring classes are receipt/provenance rewrites, stale pins/measurement identity, quote/source fidelity, and carried counter/settlement defects. Explicit open statuses include the CJK ceiling (53–55), receipt rewrite (56–57), counter limits (57–58), and crawler-identity sentence (carried since 51, open in 58); most other defects merely disappear from subsequent routed lists, so closure is unproven.
