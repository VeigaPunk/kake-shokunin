**A fabricated receipt class** looks like: a receipt whose `sha256`/`bytes`/`status` are self-consistent with a body
that was **not** produced by the stated `command` — e.g. a body hand-assembled or copied from the staged corpus
entry (which already contains the quote verbatim: `grep -cF 'The origin_summary table contains a list of all origins
in the CrUX dataset'` → 2 hits in the staged `.md`), then receipted with a matching digest. I built exactly that
fabrication in scratch (staged entry renamed to `S438-crux-bigquery.html` + a self-consistent `receipt.json`): the
quote-presence check passes and the digest matches its own bytes, yet the body was never fetched from the URL.

- **C2(c) catches:** a body that does *not* contain the located quote (`absent` fails the claim), and a quote in the wrong place/word-count — it is a **content** check on the retained body.
- **C2(c) does NOT catch:** (i) the receipt's `sha256`/`bytes`/`status` disagreeing with the bytes on disk — nothing in C2 re-derives them; (ii) the body not being the response to the URL at all (fabricated or substituted body); (iii) `status: 200` asserted rather than observed. C2(c) is satisfied by *quote-in-body*; the *provenance* of that body rests entirely on the self-reported receipt. The lane already owns the remedy pattern in `corpus/knowledge/receipt-integrity.md` (round-44/45: bind the receipt to the invocation — exit gate, counter-sha binding, run window, seed) — **C2 requires none of it.**
- **Remediation:** state in C2 that the receipt's `sha256` and `bytes` must be re-derivable from the retained file on disk (one `sha256sum`/`wc -c` line in the receipt's transcript) and that the receipt must record the fetch's observed status/URL; also require the retained body to be the HTTP response (it must contain the `origin_summary` markup — the collapsed quote is `collapsed-only`, raw grep = 0, so a collapsed body would be indistinguishable from prose the seat wrote).
