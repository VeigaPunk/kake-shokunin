# What a video-discovery case can and cannot tell us

Google's 2021 account of MX Player offers a useful example of video-discovery work, with important limits on what its results can prove. This article is an adaptation of Google's case study and current documentation, followed by twelve invented decision examples. No additional site, system or model testing was performed in preparing this lesson; no browser, Google account or search result was tested for this article. No model-weight training was performed.

## The MX Player report

In a [case study published July 29, 2021](https://developers.google.com/search/case-studies/mx-case-study), Google described MX Player as having had a minimal video web presence before adding structured data and frequently submitting video sitemaps. Over a six-month period, Google reported **more than threefold growth in traffic from Google**. It also reported a separate **100% increase in video page views per user session on the platform from organic Search traffic**.

The second measure is a per-session rate for a particular traffic source, not a count of all video views or users. A 100% relative increase means a rate doubled, but the report does not supply its numerical starting or ending value. Nor does it provide exact measurement dates, comparison windows or enough denominator detail to reproduce the results.

These reported outcomes accompanied a bundle of optimization work. The case does not describe a controlled intervention that isolates sitemaps, structured data or other changes from concurrent influences. It therefore cannot establish that sitemaps alone caused the traffic growth, quantify either action's individual contribution, guarantee another site's results, or establish MX Player's current status or whether every video was indexed.

## Turning the example into a sound review

Google's current [video SEO guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/video) distinguishes discovering a video, indexing it and showing a particular feature. For video-feature eligibility, the video must be embedded on a watch page whose main purpose is viewing one video. The watch page must be indexed and performing well in Search before its video can be considered for indexing. The video needs a valid thumbnail at a stable URL and must not be hidden behind other elements. An indexed watch page does not ensure its video is indexed, and meeting requirements or adding markup does not guarantee a search appearance.

A supplementary product video or a category page with several equally prominent videos is not a dedicated watch page. Such a page may still be eligible for a text result or a Google Images result with a video badge. Google advises against relying on a user action such as a click or swipe to load the video. These are current rules consulted November 3, 2026, not a claim about the exact rules in 2021.

A [video sitemap](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/video-sitemaps) helps Google find and understand videos, especially newly added or otherwise hard-to-discover ones. [VideoObject structured data](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/structured-data/video) describes video on a page and can also aid discovery. They are different mechanisms; neither replaces the underlying page and access requirements.

Keep four URL roles distinct:

- **Watch page:** the page embedding the video, identified by the sitemap's parent `loc`.
- **Player:** the player for that specific video, identified in a sitemap by `video:player_loc` or in structured data by `embedUrl`.
- **Media file:** the actual supported video file, identified by `video:content_loc` or `contentUrl`.
- **Thumbnail:** a valid image available at a stable URL to both Googlebot and Googlebot Images.

A sitemap video entry requires a thumbnail URL, title, description, and at least one of the media-file or player URLs. The video description must match the description displayed on the page embedding the video, though not necessarily word for word. Neither the file nor player URL may be the parent page URL. Google recommends providing the media-file URL when possible, while permitting the player URL as an alternative. Referenced sitemap URLs must be accessible to Googlebot without login, robots blocking, firewalls or metafiles, using a supported protocol. Unrelated or merely incidental videos should not be listed for a page.

For specific features such as video previews and key moments, Google must be able to fetch the actual video bytes at a stable URL. That is distinct from the sitemap's allowance for a player URL in place of a file URL. Thumbnail URLs should remain stable and be consistent across metadata sources for the same video. Google's sitemap guidance also says to omit an expiration date for a video that does not expire; when a date is supplied, Google says Search will not show that video after it. Recurring content at the same URL needs the new expiration date.

A useful evidence record keeps the four URL roles, page purpose, metadata, access assumptions and dates distinct. If comparing outcomes, record metric definitions, sources, populations, collection and comparison windows, and concurrent changes. A prepared record is not a live observation.

## Twelve hypothetical decisions

Each example is invented, and its conclusion is limited to the stated facts.

1. **Page or file?** A proposed sitemap uses a film's MP4 address as the parent page URL, though a separate watch page embeds it. **Decision:** propose the watch-page URL for the parent `loc` and the media URL for the video-file field if otherwise suitable. This correction does not establish indexing. [Video sitemap reference](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/video-sitemaps).
2. **Player without file URL.** A watch-page entry includes the required thumbnail, title and matching description, plus an accessible player URL for that specific video, but no file URL. **Decision:** the player can satisfy the sitemap's either/or URL requirement. It does not establish overall eligibility or the file-fetch access needed for previews. [Video sitemap reference](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/video-sitemaps); [video SEO guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/video).
3. **Login-blocked player.** A sitemap points to a player requiring Googlebot to log in. **Decision:** flag the conflict with the sitemap access requirement and seek an appropriate accessible endpoint. That finding alone says nothing about every other video on the site. [Video sitemap guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/video-sitemaps).
4. **Unrelated clip.** A travel article's sitemap entry lists an unrelated appliance advertisement. **Decision:** remove that clip from this page's video entry. It might have its own appropriate watch page, but that page's status is unknown. [Video sitemap guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/video-sitemaps).
5. **Click-to-load video.** A proposed watch page inserts its only video element after a visitor clicks a poster. **Decision:** flag reliance on the click and propose making the video discoverable without it. A metadata entry alone does not establish that Google can find the embedded video. [Video SEO guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/video).
6. **Supplementary product clip.** A detailed product page includes a small 360-degree clip, while its main purpose is product information. **Decision:** do not treat it as a dedicated watch page on those facts. Consider a separate genuine watch page if appropriate; the product page may still qualify for a text result or Google Images result with a video badge. [Video SEO guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/video).
7. **Unstable thumbnail.** A thumbnail URL expires every few minutes, and the sitemap and structured data give different thumbnail URLs for the same video. **Decision:** flag both issues and propose one stable, valid image URL accessible to Googlebot and Googlebot Images, used consistently in metadata. Removing those obstacles does not guarantee indexing. [Video SEO guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/video).
8. **Past expiration.** An entry says a still-available recurring video expired last month; its owner says its next expiration is next month. **Decision:** update the metadata to the actual new expiration. If the video never expires, omit the date instead. An old metadata date does not itself prove the underlying file expired. [Video sitemap reference](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/video-sitemaps).
9. **Page indexed, video unknown.** A dated record says a watch page is indexed but offers no video-indexing observation. **Decision:** report the page's indexed state as of that observation and leave the video's status unknown. Do not infer a video result. [Video SEO guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/video).
10. **Valid syntax, wrong description.** An XML parser accepts a proposed sitemap whose video description describes a different film from the one embedded on the page. **Decision:** record the syntax result only as syntax evidence, flag the mismatch and propose an accurate description. Parsing alone does not prove correctness, accessibility, indexing or a search feature. [Video sitemap reference](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/sitemaps/video-sitemaps).
11. **Several changes at once.** A publisher adds structured data and sitemaps, redesigns watch pages and changes marketing during a six-month traffic rise. **Decision:** report the association with its definitions and windows, record the concurrent changes, and do not credit the rise solely to the sitemap. Consider a separately designed controlled comparison before making a causal claim. This analytical example is not an additional result in the [MX Player case](https://developers.google.com/search/case-studies/mx-case-study).
12. **Rate versus total.** An organic-Search cohort rises from two to four video page views per user session while its session count falls by half. **Decision:** the rate increased 100%, but if these are complete comparable populations, total video page views are unchanged: twice the rate multiplied by half the sessions. These numbers are invented; MX Player's baseline and totals are not supplied. Do not turn its reported rate increase into a total-view or traffic increase. [MX Player case study](https://developers.google.com/search/case-studies/mx-case-study).
