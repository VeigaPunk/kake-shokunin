# NDTV performance: an improvement report is not a causal experiment

This article is a historical evidence exercise for publishers, not an assessment of NDTV today or any other current website. The twelve decisions below are hypothetical manual exercises, not executed tests. No live system, field metric, lab run, model test or model-weight training was performed for this article.

The case illustrates why a reported improvement, its numerical calculation, its measurement scope and its possible causes need separate treatment. It is not a universal optimization recipe.

## The historical report

Google's [web.dev case study on NDTV](https://web.dev/case-studies/ndtv), last updated 27 October 2020, reports that the publisher benchmarked Largest Contentful Paint (LCP) at **3.0 seconds at the 75th percentile**, using Chrome User Experience Report (CrUX) field data, before optimization, and **1.6 seconds after**. The first value exceeds the currently documented good LCP threshold of 2.5 seconds; the second is below it. This is a reported field-benchmark change, not an observation that every visit was slow or subsequently fast. The case says the work achieved results in about a month or within a few weeks, but does not give exact intervention or measurement dates, the collection windows, sample size, device split, country, or whether the result is URL- or origin-level. Its roughly 200 million monthly unique users is a claimed overall audience figure, not the CrUX sample. CrUX represents eligible Chrome user experiences, not necessarily all visitors; the current [CrUX methodology](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/crux/methodology) describes eligibility and aggregation. Current eligibility rules should not be silently projected back as a complete description of the 2020 sample.

The case separately reports **55% LCP improvement**. The displayed endpoints yield (3.0 − 1.6) / 3.0 × 100 ≈ 46.7% reduction. Both are source facts or direct arithmetic on what it displays; the article does not explain the discrepancy. Do not silently replace either number, infer unreported precision or a different denominator, or claim a resolution. Likewise, p75 is a distribution summary, not a per-user or every-visit guarantee.

The case describes a package of changes rather than an isolated experiment. For example, NDTV extended static-content caching to 30 days, adjusted font display so text could appear sooner, and delayed some below-the-fold advertising and social requests to give the main content priority. These examples do not establish which change produced any measured difference. The source reports CLS of 0.05 and WebPageTest grades of A for First Byte Time and effective CDN use, without giving enough detail in the case to reconstruct the CLS measurement scope or the exact grading runs. Those measures and grades are separate from the p75 field LCP result; neither an A grade nor CLS alone proves a complete Core Web Vitals pass. The case mentions FID in its historical context. Today's documented Core Web Vitals are LCP, INP and CLS, not FID; see [Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals).

The case associates the work with a **50% reduction in bounce rate**, and reports increased engagement and content consumption and proportionally increased revenue, without supplying the underlying figures or experimental design. It explicitly says NDTV made other product changes at the same time, so it cannot conclusively attribute the bounce reduction solely to Web Vitals optimization. The report also does not isolate which performance change, if any, caused the LCP change. Concurrent changes, unspecified measurement windows and no described controlled comparator limit causal inference; do not turn its before/after account into a proven causal effect. A randomized or otherwise credible controlled comparison could strengthen inference if designed and conducted, but none is described in this case.

## Preparing a defensible decision

For a prospective publisher investigation, keep baseline and follow-up records with metric definition and source, exact collection periods and timezone, page URL or origin, form factor, geography if available, population and sample/eligibility limits, relevant release dates, changes and concurrent events. Preserve distributions where available, not only a single percentile. A lab trace can help identify render-blocking resources or test a proposed change under recorded conditions; it does not establish a field result or business outcome. The [current Web Vitals guidance](https://web.dev/articles/vitals) treats these as field metrics and says lab measurement does not substitute for field measurement. Before applying a change, consider functionality, ad obligations, accessibility and layout stability as well as load timing. Record a proposed plan separately from an observed result, and obtain any permissions needed before collecting data or changing a site.

## Twelve hypothetical manual evidence decisions

Each exercise is invented; the judgment is limited to its stated facts. None asserts a current finding.

1. **Conflicting percentages.** A report quotes 3.0 s before and 1.6 s after and calls the change 55%. **Decision:** preserve the reported 55% and separately calculate approximately 46.7% from the displayed endpoints. Flag the discrepancy as unexplained, rather than inventing an underlying value or changing the source's claim.
2. **Percentile versus every visit.** A colleague says “p75 fell to 1.6 s, so every visitor loaded within 1.6 s.” **Decision:** reject the universal claim. The percentile summarizes the measured distribution, so some experiences may be slower. Ask for the distribution and its population before describing the tail.
3. **Device and URL scope.** A historical result without device or URL/origin scope is presented as the mobile result for a named article. **Decision:** reject that added specificity. Obtain the original result's form factor and URL or origin scope; origin-level data cannot by itself prove a particular article's value.
4. **A threshold versus a full pass.** A hypothetical field record shows LCP p75 of 1.6 s for a stated window and device but has no INP or CLS. **Decision:** the LCP value meets the current good LCP threshold for that recorded scope; it does not establish a full Core Web Vitals pass. Obtain the other current metrics and comparable scope before making that broader judgment.
5. **Lab improvement without field follow-up.** A controlled lab trace of the same hypothetical build and setup improves LCP after deferring a script; no post-release field data exists. **Decision:** retain it as evidence for that lab setup and a candidate fix, not as proof that real-user p75 improved. Plan a scoped field follow-up after release.
6. **A-grade diagnostics.** A WebPageTest report gives A grades for First Byte Time and CDN use, but supplies no field LCP distribution. **Decision:** report the grades as those tool diagnostics only; neither establishes a p75 LCP value or a full field pass. Seek the actual field measure.
7. **Bounce after multiple changes.** A publisher changes page speed, navigation and story recommendations together; the measured bounce rate then halves. **Decision:** describe the association and simultaneous changes, not a 50% causal effect of performance. Preserve the definition, baseline, windows and competing changes, and consider a controlled comparison before assigning credit.
8. **Audience confused with sample.** A site reports 200 million monthly unique users but does not disclose how many experiences contributed to its CrUX result. **Decision:** do not use 200 million as the CrUX sample size or claim representativeness of every browser. Report the audience claim separately and sample size as unknown; inspect eligibility when planning collection.
9. **Unspecified windows.** Two reported p75 values have no dates or collection periods, and a launch note says a change shipped sometime between them. **Decision:** record an unscoped before/after report, not a precise time-to-impact or clean pre/post comparison. Request window dates, overlap, deployment timing and other changes; do not assume today's API window was the historical source's window.
10. **Blanket ad deferral.** A proposed change delays every ad and social request until scroll because NDTV delayed some below-the-fold requests. **Decision:** do not infer that the broader rule is safe or supported by that case. First identify which requests compete with critical content and assess placement, functionality, contractual requirements and layout effects; test the candidate in an authorized setting.
11. **Attributing one bundled fix.** A hypothetical release changes cache lifetime, font behavior, resource scheduling and a CDN configuration simultaneously, then field LCP improves. **Decision:** credit the observed before/after change only to the measured population and scope, not to one component. A separately designed controlled comparison or staged rollout may distinguish contributions, subject to feasibility and authorization.
12. **Old and current metric sets.** A 2020 report says FID was already decent and gives a later CLS score of 0.05; a summary claims the site passes today's LCP, INP and CLS requirements. **Decision:** reject the current full-pass claim. FID is not today's INP, the historical CLS scope is not specified, and neither old report establishes current field data. Request current, matched field measurements for all three metrics.
